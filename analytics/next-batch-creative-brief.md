# Next-batch creative brief: the next 10 Shorts

Built from the 2026-11-15 audit (`creative-patterns.md`, `experiment-log.md`). The batch is slate cases 16–25 plus case 30, reordered and re-angled. **Every fact below is a premise from the slate or a widely reported detail. The authoring run must verify each one with at least 2 sources before scripting and drop anything that doesn't hold**, as `AUTHORING.md` already requires. Nothing here copies an earlier episode: each case keeps a new 3D world; only the principles carry over.

## What changes for all 10 ("spine v2")
| v1 (measured weak spot) | v2 |
|---|---|
| Hook → cut to Rook in his office: "No chance. Let's go look." (−30% of audience) | Hook → **Rook is already in the scene**: one line of doubt, at most 1.5 s, the danger still in frame behind him |
| "Month Year. Name, job…" jump back (−21%) | **Keep moving forward from the hook moment.** Date, place, name as a 1-second case-file label on screen, not narrated. Background only where it raises the stakes. |
| Hook names a number ("100 m", "14 km", "72 seconds") | Hook names **one physical image** a stranger can feel. Numbers go later, as proof. |
| Rook narrates from outside | Rook **does one detective action per Short in the world**: points at a clue, measures, tests, reacts in close-up. That is his signature moment. |
| Always "Case closed." | Question ending on 5 Shorts (E4); "Case closed." on the others |

**Sound (craft guidance, not yet supported by data):** silence or low room tone under the hook's first frame, one sharp story sound on the first cut (not a generic boom), music enters only once the story moves forward. Keep narration clearly above the music. Log the sound plan in `episode.json` so E-series results can later be split by it.

**First frame (E3):** odd-numbered Shorts open on a **person close-up** (face or hands, eye level, in the danger); even-numbered open on a **wide spectacle** shot. Both are made as well as possible: this tests framing, not effort.

### 1. He Cut the Rope (slate 16, Joe Simpson, Siula Grande, Peru, 1985)
- **First frame (close):** a climber's gloved hands on a rope, his face below in a headlamp, hanging over a drop. The rope goes slack.
- **Hook:** "His leg is broken. He's hanging off a cliff in the dark. Then the rope goes slack."
- **Rook in scene:** perched on the ice ledge above: "Slack? Somebody cut that. Nobody survives this."
- **Forward spine:** the fall into the crevasse → the choice to go *down*, deeper → light from a gap → days crawling across the glacier → reaching camp hours before his partner leaves.
- **Reversal:** his partner cut the rope to save himself, and that's the only reason both lived.
- **Rook moment:** he measures the crevasse with a pebble drop and gulps.
- **Ending (question):** "His partner cut the rope. Would you have?"

### 2. The Light in the Cave (slate 20, Tham Luang, Thailand, 2018)
- **First frame (wide):** a flooded cave mouth in monsoon rain, bikes parked outside.
- **Hook:** "Twelve boys and their coach went into a cave. Then the rain came, and the way out filled with water."
- **Rook in scene:** dripping on a rock inside: "Nine days in the dark? Nobody's alive in here."
- **Forward spine:** water rising → boys climb to a ledge → days of nothing → a diver's light rises out of the water → the rescue is harder than the finding.
- **Reversal:** finding them was the easy part; diving them out was the real problem. Handle the rescue diver who died during the operation with respect: no jokes from there on.
- **Rook moment:** he counts the boys on the ledge with a wingtip, comes up one short, then counts the coach.
- **Ending:** "Case closed."

### 3. A Note on the Drill (slate 21, San José mine, Chile, 2010)
- **First frame (close):** a miner's dusty hand taping a folded note to a drill bit in a lamp's beam.
- **Hook:** "Seventeen days after the mine collapsed, a drill came back up with a note taped to it."
- **Rook in scene:** on the drill rig at the surface: "A note? From 700 metres down? Let me read it."
- **Forward spine:** what the note says → 33 men, one small shelter, rationed food → the long wait → the capsule.
- **Reversal:** the 17 days before the note, when everyone above thought they were dead.
- **Rook moment:** he squeezes into a capsule mock-up and gets stuck.
- **Ending (question):** "One capsule, 33 men. Who goes up first? They had to decide."

### 4. The Hut That Floated Away (slate 19, Aldi Novel Adilang, Indonesia, 2018)
- **First frame (wide):** a tiny wooden fishing hut alone on a flat ocean at night, one lamp.
- **Hook:** "A teenager is alone in a floating fishing hut. The rope snaps, and the ocean takes him."
- **Rook in scene:** on the hut's roof: "A hut isn't a boat. He's not making it."
- **Forward spine:** drifting → catching fish, burning the hut's wood to cook → ships pass and don't stop (verify how many) → the one that does.
- **Reversal:** what finally got a ship to stop (verify the detail before scripting).
- **Rook moment:** he waves a scrap of cloth at a passing ship that sails on.
- **Ending (question):** "Ships kept sailing past. How would you get one to stop?"

### 5. Holding On (slate 22, Bahia Bakari, Yemenia Flight 626, 2009)
- **First frame (close):** a girl's hands gripping a piece of wreckage in dark, choppy water.
- **Hook:** "She's twelve, she can barely swim, and she's alone in the sea at night, holding a piece of a plane."
- **Rook in scene:** on a floating panel nearby, quiet: "Alone, all night, at twelve? I don't believe it."
- **Forward spine:** hours in the water → dawn → a boat → she was the only survivor.
- **Tone:** a tragedy. Rook stops joking from the crash onward. Do not show the crash itself in gory detail.
- **Rook moment:** he stays silent and holds the wreckage beside her until the boat comes.
- **Ending:** "Case closed."

### 6. He Fell 5 km Into a Forest (slate 24, Nicholas Alkemade, 1944)
- **First frame (wide):** a burning bomber over a dark forest at night; a tiny figure drops from the tail.
- **Hook:** "His plane is on fire, his parachute is burning, and he jumps anyway."
- **Rook in scene:** falling alongside him, wings out: "Without a parachute? Then I'm ending this case early."
- **Forward spine:** the fall → pine branches → deep snow → waking up and asking for a cigarette (verify) → the Germans who don't believe him.
- **Reversal:** his captors don't believe his story, so they check the wreck to prove it (verify the detail).
- **Rook moment:** he does the maths on a notepad mid-fall and gives up.
- **Ending:** "Case closed."

### 7. Alone on a Wrecked Yacht (slate 18, Tami Oldham Ashcraft, 1983)
- **First frame (close):** a woman waking on the cabin floor of a wrecked yacht, blood on her forehead, water sloshing.
- **Hook:** "She wakes up on the floor of a wrecked yacht. Her fiancé is gone."
- **Rook in scene:** on the snapped mast: "No mast, no engine, no radio. She's not getting home."
- **Forward spine:** what's left on board → a jury-rigged sail → finding her way by sextant (verify) → 41 days to Hawaii.
- **Tone:** grief. No jokes after the hook.
- **Rook moment:** he holds the sextant up to the sun with her, and the angles finally line up.
- **Ending:** "Case closed."

### 8. The Prisoner the Volcano Missed (slate 30, Ludger Sylbaris, Saint-Pierre, Martinique, 1902)
- **First frame (wide):** a volcano blasting a cloud over a seaside city at dawn.
- **Hook:** "A volcano wipes out a city of thousands. One of the only survivors was locked in its jail."
- **Rook in scene:** at the cell's tiny window: "A prisoner? The one man who couldn't run?"
- **Forward spine:** why he was in the cell → the blast → the heat through the window slit → days before anyone found him → what he did next (verify the circus detail).
- **Reversal:** the punishment that was supposed to hurt him is what saved him.
- **Rook moment:** he taps the thick stone wall with his beak, then the tiny door.
- **Ending (question):** "Luck, or the walls? What saved him?"

### 9. 76 Days in a Rubber Raft (slate 17, Steven Callahan, 1982)
- **First frame (close):** a man's face lit by a torch, water pouring into a cabin, then the raft canister.
- **Hook:** "His boat sinks in the night. He gets into a rubber raft with a spear gun. He'll be in it for 76 days."
- **Rook in scene:** on the raft's edge: "Seventy-six days in a balloon? It'll pop first."
- **Forward spine:** the fish that follow the raft → spearing them → the raft punctured (verify) → repairs → land.
- **Reversal:** the fish that kept him alive were also following him all the way (verify how this is told in sources).
- **Rook moment:** he patches a hole with his own feather.
- **Ending (question):** "Seventy-six days. What would you miss most?"

### 10. Eight Days by the Wreck (slate 23, Annette Herfkens, Vietnam, 1992)
- **First frame (wide):** a plane's broken wing in a green jungle valley under rain.
- **Hook:** "A plane goes down in the jungle. She's alive, injured, and nobody knows where they are."
- **Rook in scene:** on the wing: "Eight days out here, hurt? No chance."
- **Forward spine:** collecting rainwater → the leeches, the heat → hearing searchers → being found.
- **Tone:** her fiancé died in the crash. No jokes after the hook.
- **Rook moment:** he shows her how to fold a leaf to catch rain, then realises she's already doing it.
- **Ending:** "Case closed."
